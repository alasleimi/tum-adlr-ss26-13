## Goal

Deliver a scientifically coherent report and a highly visual poster about increasing the reliability of reinforcement learning.

The project asks how close a policy can get to perfect performance across a complete initial-state grid and across independent training seeds. “Chasing the nines” refers both to increasing success from 90% toward 99%, 99.9%, and beyond, and to reducing variability across states and seeds.

The final story may organize the experiments post hoc around the evidence. It must describe completed work rather than follow the temporal order of discovery.

## 26 July narrative reset

The current poster is visually polished but scientifically fragmented. This revision rebuilds the report and poster around one verified evidence chain:

1. Delayed consequences make hard swing-up states difficult to rank from a short-horizon reward target.
2. SACN8 and FastSACN8 change critic learning so that useful corrective actions can be recovered more often.
3. In the matched five-seed comparison, the FastSACN8 actors reach the torque limit more often and have lower output sensitivity than one-step SAC, even though their critics support a more reliable Q-search deployment.
4. Learner-state DAgger labels states reached after the learner's earlier actions. This targets the multi-decision recovery states on which the pure-RL actors accumulate failures.

Every step of this evidence chain must be supported by a matched ablation or a diagnostic on the selected five-seed lineages. If the existing evidence does not support one of these statements, the statement must be weakened or removed.

Behavioral interventions and white-box diagnostics are separate evidence classes. A behavioral intervention changes the action sequence and measures the resulting rollout. A white-box diagnostic measures network internals such as output saturation, output derivatives, gradients, critic rankings, dormant units, activations, or local loss geometry. The poster and report must name the exact model, checkpoint family, seeds, dataset, intervention, and comparator for each diagnostic. Behavioral sequence repair cannot be presented as mechanistic interpretation of the network.

The final deliverables tell the scientific story rather than the chronology of experiment promotion. Internal promotion gates and the P7 gate history are excluded from the report and poster because they do not explain the learning mechanism or the final comparison.

Visible report and poster language must describe the policies, evidence, and mechanism. It must not describe compliance with the request. In particular, do not use headings or claims such as "legal policy," "scope and legality," "post-gate," "plagiarism free," "clear conclusion," or similar assistant-like meta-commentary.

The deliverables must answer:

1. What is the best RL plus supervised recipe we found?
2. What is the best pure-RL recipe we found?
3. Which components helped each recipe?
4. Why does pure RL have a larger reliability tail than RL plus supervision?
5. Why did neither category reach 100% under the selected budget?

## Mainline eligibility

Every headline RL plus supervised and pure-RL result must contain five independently trained actor seeds.

One-seed and two-seed screens may motivate a promotion or support a diagnostic. They cannot become headline methods. Supervised-only evidence may be reported with fewer than five seeds only under the existing explicit exception, with the seed count and uncertainty visible.

Coordinate-agnostic critic Q-search and global physical symmetry are valid deployment operators. Reference returns, hardcoded angle ranges, angle-specific routing, checkpoint mixtures, and model ensembles are excluded at inference. These constraints belong in the reproducibility record or report methods when necessary. They must not become the poster's story.

Near-reference success is the primary selection metric. Task success, strict wins, mean return, worst-tail return, and seed variability must be reported alongside it.

## Diagnostic interpretation

Diagnostics must help explain a main result. They cannot enter the report merely because they produce an attractive plot.

The main questions are:

1. Is the pure-RL gap caused mainly by missing experience, actor optimization, critic ranking, representation loss, or sensitivity to errors accumulated across consecutive decisions?
2. Are failures shared across seeds or concentrated in seed-specific state tails?
3. Does supervision improve the same cells where pure RL is inconsistent?
4. Does FastSACN8 improve critic-guided deployment while harming the raw actor, and why?

Interpretation rules:

- A diagnostic enters the four-page body only if it separates live explanations or changes the interpretation of a winner.
- Correlational diagnostics use “associated with,” “consistent with,” or “does not support.”
- Causal language requires a matched intervention.
- Zero dormancy only rules out widespread dead units. It does not establish healthy optimization.
- A loss landscape is local to the selected checkpoint, perturbation directions, dataset, and scale.
- Reference-prefix interventions are diagnostic only and are unavailable at deployment.
- Exploratory diagnostics, raw traces, and redundant heat maps move to the annex.
- Suspect or irreproducible diagnostics are removed rather than rationalized.
