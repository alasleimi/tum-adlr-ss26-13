\begin{abstract}
An average return can hide a small but consequential failure tail. We study reliability on a controlled Pendulum benchmark with 2,501 initial states and five independently trained actor seeds. The deployed policies never query the reference. Mixed training may query reference actions and returns, while final scoring occurs only after the actor and deployment rule are frozen. Hardcoded angle routers, angle-specific inference rules, and mixtures of policy checkpoints are excluded. The selected reinforcement-learning-plus-supervision recipe obtains 12,496 near-reference successes from 12,505 seed-state trials, or 99.928\% pooled success. The selected pure reinforcement learning recipe obtains 11,832, or 94.618\%. This is an unequal-resource comparison between selected pipelines, not a causal estimate of supervision. The difference is primarily seed-dependent: the mixed method succeeds for all five seeds on 99.680\% of grid cells, compared with 90.164\% for pure reinforcement learning. Five-seed diagnostics do not support a broad reset-coverage deficit on variable cells. They instead identify a pattern consistent with early trajectory drift and imperfect critic ranking.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

This project began with a simple question: what prevents a deep reinforcement learning policy from moving from 90\% success to 99\%, 99.9\%, and the next nine? The practical target changed as the work progressed. The final study compares two routes to reliable control: a mixed route that uses reference actions during training, and a pure reinforcement learning route that learns only from reward. The goal is a policy that works across initial conditions and across random training seeds.

Reliability has two axes here. Within a seed, a policy should cover the full evaluation grid rather than only common reset states. Across seeds, the same state should not alternate between success and failure because of training randomness. This distinction matters because a pooled success rate can conceal a policy family whose difficult states change from seed to seed.

Even in a simple deterministic control task, a five-point average-rate improvement can correspond to hundreds of seed-state failures and large training-seed variability. The selected mixed pipeline reaches 99.928\% near-reference success, compared with 94.618\% for the selected pure-RL pipeline.

This pipeline synthesis and reliability audit makes four contributions. First, it audits all standardized evaluations in two experiment repositories and applies one legality rule and one evaluation protocol. Second, it identifies the best completed clean automatic-priority mixed and pure-RL pipelines among that audited candidate set at 100,000 learning transitions or fewer under the selected-route ledger. This ledger does not equalize full-study cost, discovery interactions, reference queries, or complete-pipeline replication. Third, the study decomposes both winners with paired ablations. Fourth, it combines replay support, exact-start experience reuse, first-divergence trajectories, and critic counterfactuals to test the hypothesis that pure-RL failures arise from sparse experience, poor reuse of experience, compounding actor error, or critic ranking error.

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Soft Actor-Critic.}
Soft Actor-Critic, or SAC, learns a stochastic actor and twin value critics while maximizing reward and entropy \cite{haarnoja2018sac}. Twin critics reduce optimistic value errors. In this project, categorical SimbaV2 critics represent a distribution over bounded return atoms rather than only a scalar mean.
This categorical representation inherits the C51 distributional Bellman projection \cite{bellemare2017distributional}. We use its expected return for action ranking; the project does not optimize a risk measure over the learned distribution.

\paragraph{DAgger and learner-state coverage.}
Dataset Aggregation, or DAgger, repeatedly lets the learner control the system, labels the visited states with a reference policy, and adds those examples to the training set \cite{ross2011dagger}. This procedure directly addresses compounding error under distribution shift. Our mixed pipeline adds an automatic start-selection stage that ranks uniformly sampled candidates by measured regret. This is related to automatic curricula that revisit conditions with high estimated learning potential \cite{jiang2021plr}, but our score is frozen-policy reference regret rather than temporal-difference error. The automatic stage does not use an angle interval. The initializer sampling bias is disclosed in the method and limitations.

\paragraph{Normalized residual networks.}
SimbaV2 uses hyperspherical normalization and residual structure to stabilize scalable deep reinforcement learning \cite{lee2025simba}. Both winning actors use SimbaV2 backbones, although the matched DAgger backbone ablation shows that the architecture name alone does not explain the result.

\paragraph{Multi-step SAC critics.}
SACn trains SAC critics with multi-step returns \cite{lyskawa2025sacn}. FastSACN reduces the number of active horizons. The historical FastSACN8 critic used by the mixed method activates one-step and eight-step endpoints with $\lambda=0.5$. The normalized nominal weight of the eight-step endpoint is only $0.5^7/(1+0.5^7)=0.7752\%$. It is therefore mostly a one-step critic with a small long-horizon auxiliary. This detail prevents an unsupported conclusion that eight-step credit assignment caused the mixed result.

\paragraph{Reliable evaluation and hybrid improvement.}
Deep reinforcement learning comparisons are sensitive to random seeds, implementation choices, and aggregate statistics \cite{henderson2018matters,agarwal2021precipice}. We therefore report exact paired grid counts and cross-seed cell consistency instead of only a mean and standard error. Demonstration-augmented policy gradients provide a related route for combining imitation and reward optimization \cite{rajeswaran2018dapg}. Critic-guided action optimization has also been used in large-scale continuous-control systems such as QT-Opt \cite{kalashnikov2018qtopt}. Our deployment search differs by using a fixed candidate set and an explicit twin-critic agreement gate.

\paragraph{Diagnosing data and optimization limits.}
Primacy bias and plasticity studies show that replay availability, function approximation, and optimization can fail in different ways \cite{nikishin2022primacy,nauman2024plasticity}. Berseth proposes comparing generated experience with the policy learned from that experience as a practical exploration-versus-optimization diagnostic \cite{berseth2025exploration}. We adapt that idea by replaying each terminal actor from the exact initial state of every stored training episode. Dormant-neuron measurements provide a narrower representation-health test \cite{sokar2023redo}; our registered threshold finds no dormant layer, so this mechanism stays exploratory.

The broader design space includes tail-sensitive objectives, equivariant actors and critics, uncertainty-gated action selection, and distillation of searched actions back into an actor. Our experiments isolate one representative of the curriculum, symmetry, and uncertainty-gating ideas. Reflection is a global deployment projection that enforces the known sign symmetry; it is not an equivariant actor architecture. The study does not compare risk-sensitive training objectives or complete a search-distillation experiment.

\section{Problem and protocol}

\subsection{Environment and evaluation grid}

The observation is
\[
s=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta,\dot{\theta}),
\]
and the bounded torque is $a\in[-2,2]$. Each deterministic evaluation lasts 200 steps. The grid contains 61 angles in $[-\pi,\pi]$ and 41 angular velocities in $[-1,1]$, giving 2,501 states. Five independently trained actor seeds produce 12,505 seed-state trials for each method.

The environment is Gymnasium \texttt{Pendulum-v1} under a 200-step time limit with no early terminal state. Its raw step reward is
\[
r_t=-\left(\theta_t^2+0.1\dot{\theta}_t^2+0.001a_t^2\right),
\]
where $\theta_t$ is wrapped to $[-\pi,\pi]$. The reported return is the undiscounted 200-step reward sum. Actor seed identifiers are 0 through 4. The study uses the implementation's registered SimbaV2 initialization and optimizer defaults; exact configuration files accompany the artifacts.

Two stored reference solvers provide returns $R_{\mathrm{DP}}(s)$ and $R_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(s)$. The scoring reference is
\[
R^\star(s)=\max\{R_{\mathrm{DP}}(s),R_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(s)\}.
\]
The references are a stored dynamic-programming solution and a stored controller solution evaluated on the same initial condition. They are strong comparators, not a certificate of global optimality. Strict wins demonstrate that this reference is fallible.
Near-reference success and a strict reference win are
\begin{align}
\mathcal{N}(s)&=\mathbb{1}[R_\pi(s)\geq R^\star(s)-5],\\
\mathcal{B}(s)&=\mathbb{1}[R_\pi(s)>R^\star(s)].
\end{align}
Task success requires at least 80\% of steps near upright and no failure streak longer than 50 steps. A step is near upright when $\cos\theta_t\geq0.95$ and $|\dot{\theta}_t|\leq1$.

Table~\ref{tab:reference-access} separates training supervision from deployment and scoring. The reference is absent from both winning deployments. The grid trials are correlated because neighboring states are similar. Counts describe this fixed grid exactly; they are not treated as 12,505 independent population samples.

\subsection{Legality and budget}

The deployed method cannot use a hardcoded angle range, an angle router, reference values, or a mixture of trained policy checkpoints. Automatic training-state selection based on measured performance is allowed. Q-search is allowed when the same reference-free rule is applied at every state. The selected mixed initializer used a manually biased supervised sampler. We retain the result as the best completed pipeline and disclose that bias rather than presenting the experiment as a matched uniform-sampling control.

Under the conventional selected-lineage ledger, the mixed route assigns 30,000 learner-controlled DAgger transitions, 20,000 automatic-priority DAgger transitions, and one shared 50,000-transition reward-only critic to each evaluated actor route. The priority stage also spends 800,000 simulator steps per actor seed to score candidate starts. This convention gives 100,000 learning transitions and 900,000 total simulator interactions per selected route, but it repeats the shared critic in each route. Across the actual five-actor study, the mixed method uses 300,000 learning transitions: $5\times50,000$ actor transitions plus one 50,000-transition critic. Its automatic-priority discovery cost is 4,000,000 additional interactions.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Mixed training in simple language}

The winning mixed method has five parts. A SimbaV2 actor first learns reference actions across full-circle states. DAgger then labels states reached by the actor itself. A follow-up stage samples candidate starts uniformly and trains on the largest measured mistakes. The supervised target stays equal to the reference action. At deployment, a reward-trained critic checks the actor action and four nearby alternatives. A replacement is accepted only when both critics agree.

\subsection{Mixed training details}

The width-64, two-block SimbaV2 actor is initialized from 400,000 reference-labelled states. Sixty percent use full-circle angles and velocities in $[-1,1]$. Twenty percent use full-circle angles and velocities in $[-8,8]$. The remaining 20\% use $|\theta|\leq35^\circ$ and $|\dot{\theta}|\leq1$. This manually biased initializer is the sampler limitation noted above. Training uses 80 epochs, batch size 1,024, and learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$. Three learner-only DAgger rounds add 10,000 transitions each, followed by ten training epochs after each round. The learner always executes, so the DAgger mixture coefficient is $\beta=0$. The initializer uses 43,600 optimizer steps. Its terminal epoch 110 checkpoint minimizes stored validation action MAE.

The follow-up creates 240,000 new reset-support labels. It samples 4,000 candidate starts uniformly and scores
\[
\rho(s_0)=R^\star(s_0)-R_\pi(s_0).
\]
It selects the 90 largest-regret starts and 10 uniformly chosen remaining starts, then collects one 200-step learner-only trajectory from each. The resulting 260,000-example dataset is trained for three epochs at learning rate $10^{-5}$, batch size 1,024, and 762 optimizer steps. Epoch 3 is selected over epoch 0 by a lexicographic rule: near-reference rate, targeted near-reference rate, task success, targeted mean return, then mean return. The behavior-cloning loss is
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BC}}(\theta)=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}|}\sum_{(s,a^\star)\in\mathcal{D}}
\left(\frac{\pi_\theta(s)-a^\star}{2}\right)^2.
\]
The chosen condition applies no critic shift to $a^\star$.

The inference critic is critic seed 1 at its terminal 50,000-transition checkpoint. It has two width-64, two-block categorical SimbaV2 networks and 51 atoms in $[-5,5]$. These atoms represent adaptively scaled discounted return, not the raw 200-step evaluation return. At training time, reward is divided by
\[
d_t=\max\left(\sqrt{\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(G_t)+10^{-8}},
\frac{\max_{\tau\leq t}|G_\tau|}{5},10^{-8}\right),
\]
where $G_t$ is the running discounted return. The critic uses uniform replay, batch size 256, learning start at 1,000, and critic update-to-data ratio 2. Scalar action values are distribution means,
\[
Q_i(s,a)=\sum_{j=1}^{51}p_{\phi_i}(z_j\mid s,a)z_j.
\]

For horizon $h$, the soft multi-step target contains scaled rewards, intermediate entropy bonuses, and a target-distribution bootstrap:
\[
Y_t^{(h)}=\sum_{k=0}^{h-1}\gamma^k\widetilde r_{t+k}
+\sum_{k=1}^{h}\gamma^k\alpha\mathcal{H}_{\pi}(s_{t+k})
+\gamma^h Z_{\bar\phi}(s_{t+h},a_{t+h}).
\]
The target twin is selected by the smaller scalar distribution mean. Each target distribution is projected onto the fixed support with the C51 projection $\Phi$. With $w_1=1$, $w_8=0.5^7$, and $w_h=0$ otherwise, each critic minimizes
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{L}_{Q_i}
&=\frac{1}{w_1+w_8}\sum_{h\in\{1,8\}}w_h\,\ell_i^{(h)},\\
\ell_i^{(h)}
&=-\sum_j [\Phi p_{Y^{(h)}}]_j\log p_{\phi_i}(z_j\mid s,a).
\end{align*}
The stored \texttt{fast\_last} target therefore uses only the one-step and eight-step endpoints. Critic seed 1 was selected retrospectively during the earlier standardized grid campaign and then applied to every mixed actor. There was no independent pre-registered critic-selection split. It is one critic replication rather than five.

At inference, the deterministic supervised actor proposes $a_0=\pi_\theta(s)$. The candidate set is
\[
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{local}}=
\operatorname{clip}\left(a_0+\{-0.10,-0.05,0,0.05,0.10\},-2,2\right).
\]
The proposal maximizes $\min(Q_1,Q_2)$ and is executed only if each critic values it above $a_0$. The same five-action test is used everywhere.

\subsection{Pure reinforcement learning in simple language}

The pure method trains one SAC actor and two critics from reward for 100,000 transitions. At deployment, the same actor is evaluated on the real state and its physical mirror. Their antisymmetric combination removes part of the learned left-right error. The critics then inspect 41 torques across the full action range. A replacement is used only when both critics predict an improvement greater than 0.005.

\subsection{Pure reinforcement learning details}

Each seed uses one-step SAC with a width-32, one-block SimbaV2 actor and two width-64, two-block categorical SimbaV2 critics. The replay capacity is 100,000, batch size is 256, critic update-to-data ratio is 1, $\gamma=0.99$, target update rate is $\tau=0.005$, and the actor and critic learning rates decay from $10^{-4}$ to $5\times10^{-5}$. The initial temperature is 0.01.
The temperature is optimized toward target entropy $-0.5$. Deployment uses the squashed distribution mean $a_{\rm det}=2\tanh\mu_\theta(s)$, not a sampled action. Every seed uses its terminal 100,000-transition checkpoint. The reflection and Q-search deployment rule was selected retrospectively by the completed experiment campaign and repository audit using stored standardized evaluations. The final grid therefore provides a descriptive benchmark comparison rather than a blind generalization estimate.

For sampled next action $a'$, the target is
\[
y=r+\gamma(1-d)\left[
\min_i Q_{\bar{\phi}_i}(s',a')-\alpha\log\pi_\theta(a'|s')
\right].
\]
The actor minimizes
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SAC}}
&=\mathbb{E}\left[\alpha\log\pi_\theta(a|s)-\min_iQ_{\phi_i}(s,a)\right],\\
a&=2\tanh u_\theta(s,\epsilon).
\end{align*}

For $M(s)=(\cos\theta,-\sin\theta,-\dot{\theta})$, reflection projection gives
\[
a_{\mathrm{sym}}(s)=\frac{\pi_\theta(s)-\pi_\theta(M(s))}{2}.
\]
The global candidates are $a_j=-2+4j/40$ for $j=0,\ldots,40$. The highest minimum-Q action $\hat a$ is accepted only if
\[
Q_i(s,\hat a)-Q_i(s,a_{\mathrm{sym}})>0.005
\quad\text{for }i\in\{1,2\}.
\]
This training recipe contains no FastSACN target. The completed FastSACN8 UTD2 result is a separate 50,000-step pure reinforcement learning family.

\paragraph{Learner-state follow-up.}
Adding the 20,000-transition automatic-priority DAgger stage fixes 30 near-reference trials and breaks one, for a net gain of 29. It adds seven task successes and 442 strict wins. This comparison establishes a contribution from the combined learner-state and automatic-priority follow-up. It does not isolate learner-state collection from the priority rule.

\paragraph{Automatic priority.}
Replacing uniform follow-up starts with automatic regret priority adds ten near-reference successes, two task successes, and 111 strict wins. This effect uses one follow-up actor family and is smaller than the difference between either mixed method and pure reinforcement learning. The uniform control should be emphasized when discovery cost or independent replication is the primary concern.

\paragraph{Supervised target shift.}
Tiny critic-based shifts of the reference label change zero near-reference, task, or strict classifications. Mean return changes by 0.00183. The clean no-shift target is therefore preferred.

\paragraph{Mixed local Q-search.}
The local critic corrector fixes 26 near-reference classifications and breaks none. It adds 32 task successes and loses 243 strict wins. The component improves reliability by preventing a small negative tail, rather than maximizing the probability of exceeding the reference.

\paragraph{Pure reflection.}
Adding reflection projection to the same global Q-search fixes 126 near-reference trials and breaks 33, for a net gain of 93. It adds 27 task successes and 1,197 strict wins. Applying reflection to the mixed actor loses four near-reference successes. Symmetry projection is valuable when the learned actor violates physical equivariance, but it does not provide a universal post-processing improvement.

\paragraph{Pure global Q-search.}
Adding global unanimous Q-search to the ordinary pure actor fixes 374 near-reference trials and breaks 119, for a net gain of 255. It adds 103 task successes and 40 strict wins. The harm count shows why the twin-critic gate and margin are necessary. An unconditional critic argmax exposes the policy to rare critic ranking errors.

\section{Diagnostics of the observed pure-RL failure tail}

\noindent\textbf{Evidence status: Tier B.} The analyses in this section use frozen checkpoints and matched interventions across five actor seeds. They localize error patterns but do not identify a causal training update.

\subsection{Coverage and training signal}

Reference labels supply a low-variance action target at every labelled state. DAgger labels the states that the current learner actually visits. The automatic follow-up then concentrates additional labels on measured failures while preserving a uniform component. Pure SAC sees reward-bearing transitions and must infer both action ranking and long-term credit from them. Its training signal is less direct in the states that dominate the reliability tail.

The cross-seed maps establish seed dependence: the final pure deployment has 207 variable cells and only 39 cells that fail for every seed. A new matched diagnostic then asks whether the failing seed simply received less data near the same state. For the underlying pure actors, 321 cells contain both successful and failed seeds. The failed seed has a farther nearest training reset in 51.1\% of these cells, with a two-sided sign-test $p=0.738$. Its 256-neighbor replay radius is larger in only 24.9\% of variable cells. Failed trajectories often linger in hard regions, so replay density can increase as a consequence of failure. These measurements weaken a broad missing-reset-coverage explanation.

The experience-reuse test compares each stored training trajectory with the terminal deterministic actor from the identical initial state. The final actor is better on 90.5\% of 2,500 episodes. Historical behavior wins by more than five return units on only 1.16\%. The pure learner therefore compresses most complete trajectories it generates. This result weakens a large global optimization-gap account, although it does not test whether individual corrective transitions were used effectively.

\subsection{The critic is useful but uncertain in the tail}

A stored five-seed hard-state diagnostic evaluates 99 difficult states and 21 counterfactual actions per state. Median per-state critic Spearman correlation with realized counterfactual returns is 0.693. Pairwise action ordering accuracy is 0.844. These values show useful local action information. However, the fraction of tested critic proposals that harms realized return is 0.190, and median twin-critic disagreement is 2.43 times the median local value range.

These measurements are consistent with Q-search delivering a large net gain while still breaking 119 near-reference trials. Typical rankings are useful, but harmful proposals are not rare on this targeted set: their measured fraction is 19.0\%. Only a subset crosses the near-reference classification boundary, yet those reversals determine part of the final reliability tail. The diagnostic uses stored checkpoints and a targeted state set, so it identifies an error pattern rather than a population-level causal mechanism.

The full-grid first-action intervention separates actor and critic errors. It analyzes the ordinary actors before reflection and Q-search, which produce 1,021 pure and 35 mixed failures. The final deployment rules reduce those counts to 673 and 9. A reference-like action occurs within 0.25 torque units among the 256 nearest replay actions for 98.9\% of the pure-actor failures. Replacing only the first actor action with the reference action produces some positive realized gain on 78.3\% of failures, while the critic assigns that action positive advantage on 71.3\%. A practically decisive gain above five occurs on 119 failures, or 11.7\%. The critic recognizes 64 of those 119 cases, or 53.8\%. Most failures therefore require more than one corrected initial action, and the critic misses nearly half of the strongly repairable first actions.

\subsection{Actor saturation and timing corrections}

The SAC action is $a=2\tanh u$. Its sensitivity to the pre-squash mean is
\[
\frac{\partial a}{\partial u}=2(1-\tanh^2u).
\]
Large $|u|$ makes this derivative small. On the full five-seed grid, 87.8\% of pure-actor failures start with $|a|\geq1.99$, compared with 58.4\% of successes. The reference action is also saturated on 99.4\% of those failure states, so torque saturation identifies a hard swing-up regime rather than an error by itself.

Joining these rows to the first-action intervention reveals two different failure modes. Among the 119 failures repaired by one reference action, 117 use the opposite torque sign from the reference and only 57.1\% are saturated. Their median tanh derivative is 0.00755. Among the other 902 failures, 84.9\% use the same torque sign as the reference, 91.8\% are saturated, and the median derivative is 0.000412. The first group contains gross local direction errors. The larger group is consistent with a multi-step timing error in a low-sensitivity action regime. This comparison does not prove that a particular optimizer update was blocked.

The trajectory comparison supplies a stronger timing measurement. Pure-actor failure trajectories first cross a normalized state-distance threshold of 0.1 from the stored reference at step 5 at the median. Their cumulative return deficit first exceeds five at step 35. Across the five pure seeds, failure counts range from 122 to 232 and median first-divergence steps range from 3 to 11. One-step repair counts range from 17 to 30, so the aggregate result is not produced by one seed. The 35 mixed-actor failures cross the same state threshold at step 18 and the return threshold at step 38. The small mixed failure count limits inference, but the direction is consistent with DAgger delaying compounding action error along learner trajectories.

\section{Discussion}

The strongest conclusion is empirical and benchmark-specific. The selected mixed Pendulum pipeline has better reliability across both state and seed axes than the selected pure-RL pipeline. The mixed pipeline reduces the near-reference failure count from 673 to nine and eliminates all-five-seed failure cells on the evaluation grid. The pure pipeline retains more strict wins, which shows that reward-only learning can discover excellent trajectories even while its lower tail is less controlled. Because the pipelines and resource ledgers differ, this contrast is not a causal estimate of supervision.

The ablations and diagnostics suggest a layered recipe. The learner-state result is consistent with reduced sequential distribution shift. Automatic priority provides a small additional gain. A conservative local critic corrector removes 26 more failures without breaking any near-reference classifications. In pure reinforcement learning, symmetry projection corrects errors consistent with imperfect equivariance, and global Q-search exploits critic information that is not fully expressed by the deterministic actor. The new diagnostics do not support either a broad reset-coverage deficit or a large complete-trajectory reuse deficit. Their observed pattern is consistent with delayed trajectory drift and imperfect local value ranking, but the Tier B interventions do not identify the optimizer update that created either error.

Several limitations constrain interpretation. The automatic-priority discovery stage is expensive under an all-simulator-interactions ledger. The mixed initializer oversampled a manually specified near-upright region, so its result is not a uniform-sampler control. The deployed actor and critic rule remain reference-free and angle-free. The mixed pipeline uses one selected reward-trained critic checkpoint with five actor seeds, so the actor replication count is not a five-seed replication of every component. The highest-ranked pure-RL results are mostly inference ablations on the same trained checkpoint family. The matched 100,000-step SimbaV2 plus FastSACN8 experiment is reported only if its active fifth seed and standardized evaluation finish before the integration cutoff. The diagnostic state sets are targeted and smaller than the authoritative grid. Method and checkpoint selection used the stored standardized grid campaign, which creates selection bias relative to a fresh held-out benchmark. Replication on additional continuous-control tasks, new state distributions, uniform mixed initializers, and independently trained mixed critics is required before transferring the conclusion beyond this case study.

The next experiments should target these limitations. Highest priority goes to a matched one-step versus properly weighted FastSACN8 comparison at 100,000 steps, followed by actor and critic symmetry factorials. Joint mixed training should compare behavior cloning, staged SAC, simultaneous SAC, and gradient-balanced simultaneous SAC with the same data, optimizer budget, and evaluation protocol. Automatic pure-RL hard-start curricula should rank starts only by reward or critic-calibrated performance, never by angle bands or reference regret.

\section{Conclusion}

The selected mixed method reaches 99.928\% pooled near-reference success, while the selected pure reinforcement learning method reaches 94.618\%. These unequal-resource pipelines provide the best completed benchmark results under the selected-route ledger. The mixed difference is largely a reduction in seed-dependent hard-state failures. Pure reinforcement learning still produces more strict reference wins and benefits substantially from reflection and unanimous Q-search. Five-seed diagnostics do not support a broad reset-coverage deficit and find local corrective-action support near most pure-actor failures. The observed pattern is consistent with compounding trajectory drift and critic ranking errors on the repairable tail. Chasing the next nine therefore motivates more reliable sequence learning and better calibrated actor-critic interaction across initial states and independent training seeds.

\subsection{Mechanistic diagnostic protocol}

All mechanistic comparisons use frozen checkpoints. They cannot change the reported policy or its evaluation. For the coverage test, each initial state is represented as
$[\cos(\theta)/2,\sin(\theta)/2,\dot{\theta}/2]$.
Nearest-reset distance uses the same feature map. Replay support is measured among the 256 nearest replay states for the matching seed. A replay action is called reference-like when its absolute torque difference from the stored reference action is at most 0.25.

The experience-reuse test restores the exact initial state of each stored training episode and compares its recorded stochastic return with a fresh rollout of the terminal deterministic actor. The first-action intervention executes the stored reference action once and then returns control to the frozen actor. A correction is practically decisive when this changes total return by more than five. The critic catches that correction when the minimum of its two frozen Q estimates assigns positive advantage to the reference action over the actor action.

For trajectory timing, the actor and stored reference start from the identical state. First divergence is the earliest step at which their normalized state distance exceeds 0.1. Return divergence is the earliest step at which the actor's cumulative deficit exceeds five. Thresholds were fixed before comparing pure and mixed timing, and all per-state rows are retained in the diagnostic artifacts.

\subsection{Tier C joint-loss pilot}

DAgger and SAC do not need to occupy separate chronological stages. A joint actor objective is mathematically natural:
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{joint}}=
\lambda_{\mathrm{BC}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BC}}+
\lambda_{\mathrm{RL}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SAC}}.
\]
The difficulty is scale and direction. Let $g_{\mathrm{BC}}=\nabla_\theta\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BC}}$ and $g_{\mathrm{RL}}=\nabla_\theta\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SAC}}$. Their cosine is
\[
c=\frac{g_{\mathrm{BC}}^\top g_{\mathrm{RL}}}
{\|g_{\mathrm{BC}}\|\,\|g_{\mathrm{RL}}\|}.
\]
Negative $c$ means the two updates conflict locally. Existing seed-0 screens measured negative average cosine in several joint conditions and exposed loss-scale ratios large enough for one term to dominate. A Q-filtered replay behavior-cloning arm activated on only 1.302\% of samples, producing an almost null intervention.

The 25,000-step seed-0 pilot starts from a random SimbaV2 actor and applies static reference-anchor behavior cloning and stochastic SAC in the same actor update after step 6,000. Its critic uses eight equally weighted SACn targets. It is a direct test of simultaneous loss formation, but it is not the full learner-state DAgger plus properly weighted FastSACN8 experiment. The joint actor reaches 97.681\% near-reference success, compared with 70.252\% for its matched reward-only control. This contrast shows that the supervised term helps in this seed. It cannot show whether simultaneous training is better than behavior cloning alone or a staged schedule. The pilot is excluded from the main scorecard because it does not estimate seed variability or include every factorial control.

\subsection{Coarse checkpoint policy churn}

Policy churn describes function changes on states outside the current update batch \cite{tang2024churn}. The saved checkpoints permit a coarser test at 10,000-transition intervals. Every FastSACN8 and SACn8 checkpoint is evaluated on the same 5,553 states. At 100,000 transitions, the median absolute action change from the preceding checkpoint is 0.197 torque for FastSACN8 and 0.128 for SACn8. The corresponding torque-sign flip rates are 4.72\% and 3.03\%. Median saturation grows from 32.8\% and 22.3\% at 10,000 transitions to 86.9\% and 86.7\% at 100,000 transitions, although the 20-start task-success median is nearly flat.

The result rejects a simple stopping interpretation of the small online score. The multi-step actors continue to move over the locked state set, and their action sensitivity continues to decrease. The 10,000-transition interval aliases faster changes, so this is not a per-minibatch churn measurement. Historical one-step controls saved final checkpoints only and cannot supply a matched time series.

\subsection{Matched temporal-target critic directions}

The target comparison separates actor geometry from local critic direction. Each of five one-step, FastSACN8, and SACn8 actors is paired with its own critic. On the same locked 512-state sample, the critic action derivative agrees with the realized first-action finite-difference direction on 73.1\%, 74.8\%, and 76.2\% of trials. A finite critic-directed step is harmful on 27.8\%, 26.6\%, and 24.6\%, while twin-critic gradient signs agree on 71.4\%, 78.5\%, and 81.9\%. Neither multi-step critic has worse local direction frequency under this diagnostic.

The ordinary actors' near-reference rates are 92.2\%, 76.3\%, and 78.4\%, while their saturation rates are 67.1\%, 86.8\%, and 86.8\%. Their mean discounted gains after the critic-directed step are $-0.00151$, $-0.00320$, and $-0.00286$. Better direction frequency can coexist with larger errors, actor compression, and nonlocal sequence failures. The intervention weakens a simple local-gradient explanation, while leaving critic error magnitude and actor-critic interaction open.

\subsection{Action projection limits local correction}

The preceding result counts direction, but the actor acts in a bounded space. On FastSACN8 and SACn8 failure trials, 98.5\% and 98.9\% of sampled actions are within 0.05 of the torque boundary. Their critics point farther outward in 94.6\% and 95.1\% of those boundary cases. Projection therefore reduces the nominal 0.05 critic-directed step to mean action changes of 0.00356 and 0.00347 torque. The corresponding one-step failure trials move by 0.0141 torque.

This projection bottleneck explains how a critic can identify the locally favorable sign without producing an available correction. It does not establish that saturation caused the failure. Saturated torque is often appropriate in this task, the groups are defined after observing policy outcomes, and this intervention changes only the first action. The prefix experiment shows that the larger tail requires a sustained state-dependent sequence.

\subsection{Critic recognition at the first action disagreement}

The initial-state gradient average can miss a localized critic error. Each frozen actor is therefore rolled until its action first differs by more than 0.25 from the automatically selected DP or controller reference. The reference action is substituted once at that state, then control returns to the same actor. On failure rollouts, one reference action improves 73.5\% of one-step continuations, 79.4\% of FastSACN8 continuations, and 76.5\% of SACn8 continuations. The median first disagreement occurs at steps 7, 9, and 9.

The critic ranks the reference action above the actor in 75.0\%, 51.0\%, and 68.7\% of failure cases. Among reference actions that actually improve the continuation, recognition is 86.4\%, 54.5\%, and 80.4\%. One-step and SACn8 each exceed FastSACN8 in all five paired seeds. Thresholds 0.5 and 1.0 preserve this ordering, with recognition ranges of 83.1\% to 86.4\%, 53.5\% to 54.5\%, and 78.1\% to 80.4\%. SACn8 therefore recovers much of the one-step critic's localized ranking behavior, while FastSACN8 does not. The result is post-training and outcome-conditioned. It identifies where the broad average fails, but it cannot identify which optimizer update created the critic error.

\subsection{Supervision delays the actionable error}

The same intervention is applied to every locked off-grid state for five pure one-step actors and five RL-plus-supervised actors. Pure failures first differ from the selected reference by more than 0.25 at median step 4. Mixed failures first differ at step 15. This independently reproduces the delayed mixed-policy drift seen on the authority grid, where the registered state-divergence medians are 5 and 18.

The critic does not explain this delay. Among reference actions that improve the actor continuation, the pure critics recognize 70.3\% and the shared mixed critic recognizes 70.4\%. The median action gap is 3.94 torque for pure failures and 1.38 for mixed failures, with opposite action signs in 78.1\% and 44.6\%. One reference action is beneficial in 70.0\% of the 2,259 pure failures but only 48.2\% of the 56 mixed failures. The mixed actor is at the torque boundary at the first disagreement in 17.9\% of failures, compared with 75.4\% for pure RL. The selected mixed pipeline is associated with later, smaller actor-reference divergence. This comparison cannot isolate a single training component because the pipelines differ and the mixed failure count is small.

\subsection{Critic recall does not imply critic calibration}

The preceding comparison counts how often a positive critic advantage recognizes an action that improves the continuation. That recall omits false positives and ignores the ordering of critic margins. On the 2,259 pure-RL failure rows, the sign test has 78.2\% precision, 70.3\% recall, and 62.3\% balanced accuracy. The critic margin has ROC AUC 0.655 for separating helpful from harmful reference actions and Spearman correlation 0.272 with realized gain.

On the 56 remaining mixed failures, recall is again 70.4\%, but precision is 51.4\% and balanced accuracy is 54.2\%. The pooled ROC AUC is 0.324 and the Q-to-gain rank correlation is $-0.417$. Seedwise estimates vary sharply because four mixed seeds contribute only five to ten failures each. These measurements provide no evidence that better calibration of the shared mixed critic creates the delayed actor drift. They strengthen the actor-trajectory interpretation while preserving an important limitation: the failure sets and actor pipelines differ, and the mixed estimate is small.

\subsection{Does DAgger alone delay drift?}

The five clean-DAgger actors use a width-32, one-block SimbaV2 backbone and 100,000 learner-state labels. Their pooled failure disagreement median is step 8, compared with step 4 for pure RL and step 15 for the final mixed actors. Clean DAgger therefore delays the first large disagreement modestly. The delay is not sustained: at step 16, only 1.4\% of clean-DAgger failures have not exceeded the 0.25 gap, compared with 2.0\% for pure RL and 46.4\% for the final mixed pipeline. The final actors also use a width-64, two-block backbone, 690,000 labels, reset support, and a different selection pipeline. This frozen-policy comparison therefore motivates the matched joint-loss factorial but cannot assign the sustained delay to one component.
