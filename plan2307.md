## Proposed scientific story

### One-sentence poster claim

The last mile toward a perfect policy is a reliability problem across initial states within each trained seed and across independent training seeds: pure RL learns solutions for almost the entire state space, but supervision makes those solutions more consistent, while symmetry and conservative Q-search repair part of the remaining pure-RL tail.

This is a provisional claim. It stays only if every part survives the evidence audit and the remaining matched experiments.

### Narrative arc

#### Act 1: Define “perfect”

- Success is not average return on random episodes.
- A perfect policy should work across the complete evaluation grid.
- The primary metric is near-reference success.
- Task success and strictly beating the reference show important but different behavior.
- The policy must stay clean: no hardcoded training regions, no hardcoded inference router, and no reference at inference.
- The main comparison uses at least five independently trained actors and at most 100k learning transitions.

#### Act 2: Establish the two strong baselines

- Pure RL can achieve high task success and frequently beat the stored reference.
- RL plus supervision is much more uniform over states and seeds.
- The report should show both strengths. It should not describe one category as universally better.

#### Act 3: Identify the actual gap

The current five-seed evidence suggests:

- Most pure-RL failures are not states that every seed finds impossible.
- Pure RL has a seed-dependent failure tail.
- The mixed method removes almost all of that tail.
- Pure RL can be better than the reference on many successful states, so strict wins and reliability are not the same objective.

The main diagnostic question is therefore:

> Why does reward-only training discover good behavior but fail to make it consistent across seeds and difficult initial states?

#### Act 4: Test explanations, not just correlations

Use a small sequence of diagnostic questions:

1. **Is the gap shared across seeds or seed-dependent?**
   Use cross-seed cell consistency and paired failure maps.
2. **Is the actor or critic responsible on hard states?**
   Compare actor actions, critic-ranked proposals, and rollout-tested counterfactual actions.
3. **Does the critic have useful signal but insufficient tail reliability?**
   Measure action-ranking correlation, twin disagreement, calibration, and false-positive Q-search proposals separately on successful and failed states.
4. **Does training stop improving the actor before the critic becomes useful?**
   Use checkpoint-aligned actor, critic, entropy, saturation, and evaluation curves.
5. **Does supervision fix the same cells where pure RL is inconsistent?**
   Compare paired state-by-state outcomes rather than only aggregate rates.
6. **Can better temporal targets or a proper joint DAgger loss close the gap?**
   Use the matched FastSACN8, SACn8, and joint-loss experiments below.

#### Act 5: Show what actually helped

The poster should emphasize only interventions supported by matched evidence:

- SimbaV2 compared with the appropriate architecture control
- Reflection compared on the same frozen actors
- Q-search compared on the same frozen actors and critics
- Uniform versus automatically prioritized DAgger data
- No-shift versus critic-shifted supervised targets
- One-step SAC versus FastSACN8 versus SACn under matched training
- BC-only versus SAC-only versus staged versus true joint training

#### Act 6: End honestly

The conclusion should state:

- The best clean policy found
- The best clean pure-RL policy found
- The quantified performance gap between them
- Which explanation is strongly supported
- Which mechanisms have only suggestive evidence
- Whether “perfect” was reached under the stated budget and simulator-call ledger

## Priority 2: correctly trained DAgger plus FastSACN8

FastSACN is a critic-training method, not merely an actor backbone. The correct test jointly trains:

- A randomly initialized SimbaV2 actor
- Randomly initialized SimbaV2 distributional critics
- A FastSACN8 critic objective on reward transitions
- A DAgger behavior-cloning objective on automatically collected learner states
- The SAC actor objective and DAgger objective in the same actor update

The critic objective is:

\[
L_Q=L_{\mathrm{FastSACN8}}.
\]

The actor objective is:

\[
L_\pi
=
\lambda_{\mathrm{BC}}L_{\mathrm{BC}}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{SAC}}L_{\mathrm{SAC}}.
\]

### Required matched controls

| Arm | BC actor loss | SAC actor loss | FastSACN8 critic | Schedule |

| D0 | Yes | No | Yes | BC-only actor control |

Start all arms from matched random initializations and use identical online transitions wherever the design permits.

### Loss balancing

The earlier pilot sometimes had a weighted BC-to-SAC gradient ratio near 17:1 and frequent negative gradient alignment. A nominal joint loss is not a fair joint test if one term dominates.

For D4, balance the weighted actor gradients so that:

\[
\frac{\lVert \lambda_{\mathrm{BC}}g_{\mathrm{BC}}\rVert}
{\lVert \lambda_{\mathrm{SAC}}g_{\mathrm{SAC}}\rVert}
\]

stays within a preregistered range around one. Log the unclipped ratio, applied multiplier, gradient cosine, and conflict frequency. Treat PCGrad as a separate ablation, not as part of the default joint method.

### Tier B: report-main diagnostic evidence

These diagnostics can support the mechanism discussion if they are multi-seed, reproducible, and checkpoint-aligned:

1. Critic action-ranking accuracy on frozen hard states
2. Twin-critic disagreement relative to the local Q-action signal
3. Calibration and false-positive correction rates for Q-search
4. Actor saturation, entropy, critic loss, and real evaluation performance over aligned checkpoints
5. Failure timing and first-divergence analysis on matched pure and mixed trajectories
6. DAgger data coverage compared with the states where pure RL is inconsistent
7. Joint BC and SAC gradient norms, ratios, and cosine alignment from the new controlled experiment
8. SACn horizon weights and effective sample size from the matched target comparison

No diagnostic enters the main report merely because a plot already exists. It must answer one of the six questions in Act 4.

### Tier C: annex-only exploratory evidence

Keep these available, but do not let them carry the main conclusion:

- Single-seed parameter-space loss landscapes
- Three-direction perturbation studies
- Dormancy under one chosen activation threshold
- Effective-rank and feature-norm screens
- Bellman-horizon mismatch probes
- Single-seed or ten-episode joint-loss pilots
- Specialized saturation-aware and failure-mining pilots
- Unpromoted Q-distillation and gating screens
- Large collections of individual heat maps

These can motivate hypotheses or document negative results. They cannot be described as explaining the gap without stronger confirmation.

### Tier D: quarantine or remove

Do not use a diagnostic in the report or poster if any of the following is true:

- The checkpoint or dataset cannot be identified exactly.
- Independent actor seeds are confused with evaluation RNG seeds.
- The plotted number cannot be regenerated from stored rows.
- The comparator changes several uncontrolled factors.
- A result was selected after inspecting the authority grid.
- The denominator, state set, or seed set differs without being disclosed.
- A mechanistic claim is inferred only from a run name, summary prose, or missing telemetry.
- The plot contradicts the standardized result ledger.

If an existing statement cannot pass this audit, remove it. Do not soften an unsupported statement merely to keep it.
